## 1. Market finding and decision discipline

At the stated prices, **the supported market signal is completeness of bath programs and deliberate finish hierarchy, not a particular tile trend**. Closest Old Town-boundary properties describe primary baths with strong shower/tub/vanity programs and some decorative secondary bath treatments. This supports making the existing 1529 wet areas, tub alcoves and powders photograph as intentional and coherent. It **does not** establish that adjacent buyers prefer blue over copper, subway over large format, relief over flat tile, polished brass over Black Onyx, 2×2 mosaics, a specific grout color, or an expensive slab treatment.

One independent building can contribute several recorded sale prices but **only one substantially repeated bath-design precedent** when its listing copy is duplicated. The 405 W Armitage four sales are **ONE** independent design group, not four confirmations of a tile style. Likewise the 1942 N Orchard three sales are **ONE** adjacent design group. This sample is a *directional buyer-review comparator*, not a transaction-adjusted valuation or empirical return-on-tile-investment model.

**Practical market recommendation:** Allocate design attention in this order: (1) two **primary** bath compositions and drain/floor/wall continuity, (2) legible secondary wet-area detailing, (3) one controlled accent on each **dry** powder wall, (4) minimal transitions in shared WCs. Do **not** increase the number of tile colors, shower niches, accent strips, or fixture selections merely to imitate unverified competitor photos. The real upgrade is a carefully surveyed, certified, built and photographed scheme; **BEST** need not use more material types than **BETTER**.

## 4. GOOD / BETTER / BEST — tier meanings, not approved price tiers

**GOOD (carried-reference design):** Original source palette and its dated per-unit **material-only** pricing; useful as a control for design scope and relative presentation but **may contain a failed wet-wall/floor gate** and must never be called installable solely because it appears in the v4 schedule.

**BETTER (market-legible / lower needless complexity):** Manufacturer-evidenced, more coherent wall/focal arrangement and a credible **conditional** floor format for the actual tub, linear drain or point drain. BETTER still may fail exact measured floor wet DCOF, quote, room dimensions or owner-selection gates. It is not automatically the prime agent's selected champion.

**BEST (release-quality design intent, not premium material spend):** A single refined viewpoint with measured cuts, verified compliant SKUs, suitable substrate/membrane, actual fixture/finish integration, current Chicago quote and owner approval. A bounded natural-stone focal-plane study is **optional only** where owner-authorized and costed, and supported merely by **adjacent** G3/G8/G9 listing language. No unrequested slab, tub, shower, sink, niche or wet-wall relief is incorporated by this document.

**Complexity ceiling:** **C0** = no new tile SKU, shared field/transition only; **C1** = one principal color/texture with precise joints and perhaps one existing inset; **C2** = at most one *expressly owner-approved and wet-suitable* premium focal treatment plus coordinated base finish. **C3** (several independent tile statements competing in one small bathroom) is **not recommended** on evidence presently available. The ceiling is a communication/visual discipline, not an installation-code requirement.

The following source-dollar figures are **historical September 3, 2026 ELM material rate extensions per room per single unit**, not current supplier offers, budget caps, purchase instructions or installed rates. Source quantities already include approximately **15% waste**. **Do not add another 15%** or count a quoted niche's occupied wall face twice. **PH/TX WC flooring is already in the respective Primary floor quote.** Market-confidence for all *tile-specific competitor precedents* is **P0**, even when SHB own proposed manufacturer SKU is known.
