## Baseline, differentiation and uncertainty

**Demonstrated recurring *program*, not tile design:** among multiple independent buildings, premium primary baths repeatedly advertise double vanities, stand-alone soaking tubs, separate walk-in showers, and frequently steam/heat (OT01–OT05, LP01–LP05). This aligns with target-buyer expectations at $2.7M–$2.8M. Because marketing copy can repeat development-wide, **do not calculate prevalence across 14 unit rows**: there are only nine distinct building groups in this sample.

**Demonstrated material differentiators:** a full natural-stone bath claim at 1942 N Orchard (LP01–LP03), named Calacatta Gold slab surfaces at 10 E Delaware (GC01), and natural quartzite/granite bath finishes after renovation at 270 E Pearson (GC02). These are three independent source groups. Marketing terms 'spa bath', 'luxury finishes' and 'designer' are excluded as tile evidence. Nothing in these texts establishes a normal grout joint, typical tile size, or common fixture finish.

**Top-third differentiation:** GC01 (near top of sample price range) uses named premium stone slabs and custom teak bathtub. LP01–LP03 advertise all-natural-stone baths. This suggests that a deliberately composed focal stone treatment may communicate differentiation, **without substantiating a whole-room slab requirement**.

**Dated or overdone:** no evidenced conclusion. No authenticated photos in this pass show a dated layout or color; such assessments require photo review. Equally, no statement about matte black vs brushed brass vs chrome share is justified: metal colors are unspecified.

## Five actionable implications for 1529 Wieland

1. **PH and TX primary baths:** retain a confident focal material and spatially clean, carefully cut wet-wall layout; the *program* competition includes steam shower, soaking tub and dual vanity in OT01–OT05, LP01–LP04. Verify what the actual 1529 fixtures provide rather than adding unsupported scope.
2. **Primary accent / upgrade test:** consider one clearly bounded slab or natural-stone focal plane as a *challenger*, costed against the actual schedule. LP01–LP03, GC01 and GC02 support elevated natural-material positioning, but not any particular stone selection, grout or nominal size.
3. **Secondary full baths:** give the shower/wet-room treatment a coherent, durable identity rather than relying on added ornament: OT01–OT04 promote a double-vanity Jack-and-Jill wet room, while LP03 markets independent ensuite bathrooms. Verify shower-floor wet listing and DCOF from manufacturers; comps cannot establish those technical properties.
4. **Powders and hall baths:** explore decorative dry-wall texture or wallcovering adjacent to precise tile transitions rather than gratuitous all-wall stone: OT01–OT04 explicitly describe wainscoting and designer wallcovering in the hall bath. Keep every finish inside the owner's selected plumbing vocabulary.
5. **Evidence gate before judging format, color, grout or metal:** obtain attributable bath photos/brochures and verify which surfaces they show. Until then, do not score proposed subway, terrazzo, ribbed porcelain, bookmatched slab, any grout palette or plumbing finish as 'market standard' using these comps. Recheck market/source information on later rounds.

## Limitations and next evidence needed

- **Old Town supply:** only five boundary / Old Town Triangle unit sales in this sample, four in one building; downtown Lincoln Park and Gold Coast are flagged substitutes, not pooled as equivalent micro-market transactions.
- **Source dependence:** photos were not opened and authenticated; marketing descriptions may describe the building rather than individual as-built bathrooms. Bath-specific source confidence is limited to the explicit text.
- **Missing fields:** per-bath photos, tile color/size/layout/coverage, shower-floor treatment, niches, grout and plumbing finishes; areas for OT05 and LP01–LP03 not reported. Nulls in JSON are intentional.
- **Preconstruction:** LP06 is a published list price, not a completed dwelling or closed transaction; it should be excluded from sold-price and observed-material calculations.
- **Comparable transactions:** prices are asking/sale figures, not quality-adjusted appraisals. Multiple sales within one development provide pricing corroboration only, not independent bath-design trends.
