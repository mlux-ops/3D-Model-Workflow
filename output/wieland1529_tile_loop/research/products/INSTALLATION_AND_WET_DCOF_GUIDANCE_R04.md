# Installation and floor-slip evidence addendum — 2026-11-06

## Source hierarchy and intended use

1. **Daltile**, [DCOF AcuTest and reading test results](https://www.daltile.com/why-daltile/industry-standards/dcof-slip-resistance-testing-reading-test-results) (reviewed 2026-11-06): A326.3 wet DCOF of 0.42 or greater is the ANSI reference for **level interior surfaces walked when wet**, not a guarantee that a person cannot slip. Daltile expressly says selection depends on the end-use environment, slope, footwear, expected contaminants, maintenance, texture and drainage. **Smaller tile increases grout-joint drainage and allows easier slope formation.** Therefore, the project's stricter BOARD requirement of an actual published product-specific numeric wet DCOF of at least 0.42 is a project acceptance gate; a maker's **Target DCOF Wet ≥0.42** field is NOT the measured exact SKU result. For a sloped shower, check the maker's separately designated **Shower Floors** application AND the project wet DCOF evidence; do not infer 0.42 alone ensures shower-pan safety.
2. **Daltile**, [Portfolio Cream PF07](https://www.daltile.com/products/concrete-look/portfolio/cream) and [Portfolio Ash Grey PF05](https://www.daltile.com/products/concrete-look/portfolio/ash-grey) (reviewed 2026-11-06): 12×24 matte level field and 2×2 matte straight-joint mosaic are included in product selectors; application table says Wet & Level Floor and Shower Floors suitable with **Target DCOF Wet ≥0.42**. The source does **not** show an actual measured numeric result separately for PF07/PF05 12×24 and 2×2 matte. **Result null; project floor gate FAIL until exact independent or manufacturer test document.** Manufacturer recommends **1/8-in grout joint** for these selections, but final joint must account for actual tile dimensions and setting flatness.
3. **Tile Council of North America**, [Grout FAQ](https://tcnatile.com/resource-center/faq/grout/) (reviewed 2026-11-06): joint spacing varies with actual tile dimensional variation, geometry, edge form, substrate plane and application; there is no universal fixed joint size suitable for all tiles. A nominal 1/8-in target must therefore be checked against actual received cartons and edge tolerances, with manufacturer limitations controlling.
4. **Schluter**, [KERDI-BOARD-SN niche shelf installation FAQ](https://www.schluter.com/schluter-us/en_US/faq/shower-system-kerdiboard-shower-niche-shelf-install) (reviewed 2026-11-06): maker describes embedded shelf support with compatible thin-set and permits raising the back or setting the tile on a slope to avoid water pooling. The niche product and membrane must be selected for the actual construction, and **waterproof returns/jambs and sill details are mandatory to coordinate**. Do not assert that every prefabricated niche requires a specific 2% slope: [Schluter DESIGN-NICHE FAQ](https://www.schluter.com/schluter-us/en_US/faq-does-the-schluter-design-niche-have-slope) distinguishes niche shelf slope from the shower floor/bench requirement.
5. **Custom Building Products**, [grout-joint and large-format offset note](https://www.custombuildingproducts.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/tb87-grout-joint-width.pdf) (manufacturer technical bulletin; legacy, not an installation drawing): recommends avoiding more than a one-third offset for tiles with long edges above 18 in. Because exact wall elevations here are provisional, use **aligned zero-offset 24×48** as the conservative candidate and require substrate-flatness verification, manufacturer mortar/panel approval, exact finished wall dimensions and actual cut-sheet before install.
