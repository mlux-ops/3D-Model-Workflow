**How lux is read:** the Phase 1 card and capture camera, with one change. In this scene a capture clamps every value at 1.0, so the scene's own brightness (thousands of units in daylight) cannot be read directly as it was in the empty Phase 1 level. The capture reads the exposed picture before tone mapping instead, and that value times 2 to the power of the exposure value gives the scene brightness back; checked at three exposures (EV 0, 1 and 3) against the direct reading under a dim test sun: equal to four digits, so the reading does not depend on the exposure. Lux = scene brightness of the card x 1000 / 153.375 (the Phase 1 calibration of this card and camera).
**Readings with the sun at its first value of 100,000 lux:** flat card in open air 55 m above the lot 58,872 lux; the same card with the sun hidden by a disc (sky alone) 5,362 lux; card facing the sun 87,192 lux; flat card on the sidewalk in front of the building 57,956 lux. So the engine's atmosphere lets 74 percent of the sun through at this height of sun, and the sun's lux value is the light before the atmosphere.
**Set:** sun **140,000 lux**. **Readings after:** flat card in open sun **82,482 lux** (target 80,000 to 100,000); sky alone 7,512 lux; card facing the sun 122,190 lux; flat card on the sidewalk 81,225 lux. Record: `review\phase8\brightness_readings.json`.
**Exposure, step 8.3:** automatic exposure is off in `LGT_Exposure_Outdoors`; the exposure is fixed as a camera setting, ISO 100, f/8, 1/400 second, which is **EV 14.64** (the photographer's "sunny 16" setting; plan: 14 to 15). At this value an 18 percent grey card lying in open sun comes out at 0.178, where middle grey is 0.18. **Sidewalk concrete:** the sunlit sidewalk surface itself, read 3 m from the card over a patch about 15 cm wide, comes out at 0.175, which is **0.04 stop under middle grey**. That means the Phase 7 sidewalk material reflects about 18 percent of the light; broom-finished concrete in use usually reflects 25 to 35 percent, which would sit about half a stop to one stop above a grey card. This is a property of the material, not of the lighting, and is listed for the owner; the lights are not bent to hide it.
